## Origins: what did Wertheimer, Köhler and Koffka actually state?

### Takeaway
Wertheimer (1923) is the real source of the classic grouping factors. He presented them as interacting factors with variable strength, not as a fixed list of rules. Several familiar labels, including "continuity," "common fate" and the tidy set of "seven laws," are later translations or textbook packaging.

### Cited Findings
- [REVIEW] Wertheimer's 1912 phi-motion paper is treated as the start of Gestalt psychology. Observers saw motion at intervals of about 60 ms, and at shorter intervals saw pure motion with no object moving. He concluded that structured wholes, not sensations, are the primary units — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [PRIMARY] The factors named in the 1938 translation of Wertheimer 1923 are:
  - Proximity (Sec. I)
  - Similarity (Sec. II)
  - Uniform Destiny (Sec. IV)
  - Objective Set (Sec. VI)
  - Direction (Sec. VII)
  - Closure (Sec. VII)
  - the "Good Curve" (Sec. VII)
  - past experience or habit (Sec. VIII)

  A footnote calls similarity a special instance of the Factor of the Good Gestalt — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [PRIMARY] He does use "law" ("general law," "intrinsic laws," and the translated title), but the working term is "factor," and the translation also says "principles" — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [PRIMARY] Factors can cooperate or oppose each other, and he varies their strength to see which wins. He reports a case where "similarity is victorious despite the preferential status given to proximity" (Sec. III). Intermediate cases are more ambiguous — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [PRIMARY] Past experience is listed as a candidate factor but criticised as an explanation that "brushes aside the real problems of apprehension much too easily" (Sec. VIII) — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [PRIMARY] "Prägnanz" appears through "Prägnanzstufen" (stages of figural stability), and a translator's footnote says the term cannot be translated — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [PRIMARY] Sec. IX discusses a figure segregating from a homogeneous ground, but the paper does not offer "figure-ground" as one of its factors — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [REVIEW] The review's list for 1923 is proximity, similarity, uniform density, common fate, direction, good continuation, and whole properties such as closure, equilibrium and symmetry. Parallelism is also listed in its grouping section. Prägnanz is described as the most general principle: the percept takes the simplest and most encompassing structure conditions allow. Köhler (1920) linked this to physical stability — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [REVIEW] On common fate, Wertheimer wrote that it "is valid in a wide range of conditions; how wide is not yet investigated here" (p. 316) — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [REVIEW] The review also corrects common misreadings. Gestalt theory emphasised autonomous laws but did not claim that all laws are innate or that learning plays no role. Köhler's electrical field theory was undermined by Lashley and Sperry, though pre-attentive organisation later gained neurophysiological support — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)

### Inferences
- "Wertheimer's laws" is defensible as attribution, but "laws" oversells them. The accurate wording is "factors that bias organisation and trade off against each other."
- "Good continuation" as a single modern term covers at least the 1938 translation's Direction and Good Curve. "Common fate" corresponds to that translation's "Uniform Destiny." Search older texts under these older labels.
- The translation's section list and the review's list differ, and uniform density is one example. Cite the factor list with a named edition. I did not check the German or the Spillmann translation.

### Gaps
- Koffka (1935) and Köhler's original wording were not read. [MEMORY] The Prägnanz formulation usually attributed to Koffka is in *Principles of Gestalt Psychology*, and Koffka is usually credited with saying the whole is "something else" or "different" rather than "greater" than the sum of its parts. Verify both with Koffka or Part II before use.
- [MEMORY] Wertheimer 1912 is "Experimentelle Studien über das Sehen von Bewegung," *Zeitschrift für Psychologie, 61*, 161-265. Not checked this session.

## Principle by principle: first source and definition

### Takeaway
The classic factors trace to Wertheimer, and the newer ones have specific named sources. Figure-ground is a separate problem from grouping, with its own determinants and evidence.

**Classic factors**

**Later principles**
- [ABSTRACT] **Common region.** Palmer, S. E. (1992). Common region: A new principle of perceptual grouping. *Cognitive Psychology, 24*, 433-447. Elements inside the same bounded area tend to group, and citing work says Palmer argued this cannot be reduced to proximity or closure — [Palmer et al. 2003 and citing papers via search](https://palmerlab.berkeley.edu/pdf/PalmerBrooks&Nelson.pdf). [REVIEW] Faster responses within a shared region were shown with the repetition discrimination task — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] **Uniform connectedness (UC).** Palmer, S. E., & Rock, I. (1994). Rethinking perceptual organization: The role of uniform connectedness. *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 1*(1), 29-55. A connected region of uniform properties (luminance, colour, texture, motion) strongly tends to be organised as a single unit — [Kimchi 2000](https://newiipdm.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Kimchi_2000_VisionResearch.pdf); [typeset record](https://typeset.io/pdf/uniform-connectedness-and-classical-gestalt-principles-of-3m33wfdvud.pdf)
- [REVIEW] UC was proposed as the initial partitioning process. Peterson (1994) disputed it as an entry-level unit, and Kimchi (2000) found collinearity and closure at least as important — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [REVIEW] **Element connectedness.** Palmer & Rock (1994), with objective support from Palmer & Beck (2007). Humphreys & Riddoch (1993) reported that a Balint's patient could discriminate colour arrays when paired circles were linked — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] **Synchrony.** Lee, S.-H., & Blake, R. (1999). Visual form created solely from temporal structure. *Science, 284*(5417), 1165-1168 — [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/c168a713c1a09fe024a819acaba4315c). [REVIEW] The mechanism is disputed, and Farid & Adelson argue some effects are stimulus artefacts — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/); [Adelson & Farid 1999](https://persci.mit.edu/pub_abstracts/farid_science_99_abs.html)
- [ABSTRACT] **Generalized common fate.** Sekuler, A. B., & Bennett, P. J. (2001). Generalized common fate: Grouping by common luminance changes. *Psychological Science, 12*(6), 437-444. DOI 10.1111/1467-9280.00382. Elements group by shared luminance change without physical motion. The authors separate this from temporal synchrony and treat it as an extension of Wertheimer's factor — [McMaster record](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/212418)
- [ABSTRACT] **Ordering principles.** I did not find a primary source that treats "ordering" as a named Gestalt principle in the sources read. See Gaps.

**Figure-ground determinants** [REVIEW, all from [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)]
- Convexity: seen as figure on about 90% of trials (Kanizsa & Gerbino 1976). The rate rose from 57% in two-region displays to 89% in eight-region displays at 100 ms (Peterson & Salvagio 2008), so context matters.
- Symmetry: seen as figure on about three-quarters of trials (Peterson & Gibson 1994a).
- Lower region: the region below a border tends to be figure (Vecera, Vogel & Woodman 2002). Retinal rather than environmental coordinates dominate (Vecera 2004).
- Top-bottom polarity: regions wider at the bottom are more often figure (Hulleman & Humphreys 2004).
- Extremal edges: the side with the gradient is almost always seen as closer (Palmer & Ghose 2008).
- Edge-region grouping: six grouping factors all shifted figure assignment in the predicted direction (Palmer & Brooks 2008).
- Attention: attended regions are more likely to become figure (Baylis & Driver 1995).
- Articulating motion: dynamic concavities can override convexity or symmetry (Barenholtz & Feldman 2006).
- Past experience: upright familiar objects are more often figure than inverted ones, even at masked exposures of 28 ms (Peterson et al. 1991; Peterson & Gibson 1994a).

### Inferences
- A complete skill should treat figure-ground as depth and ownership assignment at edges, and grouping as a separate question of what belongs together. Common design lists blur the two.
- Convexity's rise with region number shows that single figure-ground percentages do not transfer as constants.
- Synchrony is the weakest of the later additions to build on, because its mechanism and some of its stimuli are contested.

### Gaps
- [MEMORY] Rubin (1915) is the conventional source for figure-ground, and Rock & Brosgole (1964) and Palmer, Neff & Beck (1996) are conventional sources on grouping occurring after constancy and amodal completion. These were not verified in the text read.
- [MEMORY] "Parallelism" is also often credited to Palmer's later textbook treatment. Its exact first source was not resolved.
- "Ordering principles" is unresolved. Brooks 2015 is the best place to check, and it could not be read.
- Whether Brooks's chapter is in handbook Section 1 or Section 2 is uncertain, because the [Penn catalogue](https://find.library.upenn.edu/catalog/9977599122903681) and the OUP page reportedly disagree. Cite by chapter title.

## Modern quantitative work: Kubovy, Elder and Goldberg, Peterson, Kimchi

### Takeaway
Proximity is the best-quantified factor, with lawful and replicated numbers for dot lattices and natural contours. Proximity and similarity combine additively in the log-odds of a grouping choice in tested lattices. None of these numbers is a design spacing rule.

### Cited Findings
- [REVIEW] Oyama (1961): time-seen ratios follow a power law, th/tv = (dh/dv)^-α, with α about 2.89 — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [REVIEW] Kubovy & Wagemans (1995) and Kubovy, Holcombe & Wagemans (1998), with 300 ms dot-lattice displays:
  - Grouping along the longer-distance orientation is almost never seen when the distance ratio exceeds about 1.5.
  - Log-odds fall on a single attraction function, linear in log space, whose slope is a person-dependent sensitivity to proximity.
  - This is the **pure distance law**: grouping depends on relative distance, not overall lattice configuration. Proximity is approximately scale-invariant.

  — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [REVIEW] Kubovy & van den Berg (2008, *Psychological Review, 115*(1), 131-154, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.115.1.131): indifference curves for proximity and similarity are parallel in log-odds space, so the effects are additive. Claessens & Wagemans (2005) reported similar results for proximity and collinearity with Gabor elements — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/). The [UVA page](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/michael-kubovy/publications/whole-equal-sum-its-parts-probabilistic-model-grouping-proximity-and) lists the author order as Berg & Kubovy, while the assignment and other sources use Kubovy & van den Berg. Check at the DOI.
- [REVIEW] Rush (1937) reported "Similarity equals about 1.5 cm of Proximity," but the review says this method cannot measure relative strength. Hochberg & Silverstein (1956) favoured additivity after reanalysis but produced only one equilibrium curve — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] Elder, J. H., & Goldberg, R. M. (2002). Ecological statistics of Gestalt laws for the perceptual organization of contours. *Journal of Vision, 2*(4), 324-353. DOI 10.1167/2.4.5. Observers traced contours in natural images. Proximity, good continuation and luminance similarity are roughly independent once properly defined, supporting a simple factorial model — [PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/12678582); [Scilit](https://scilit.com/publications/0d633f12804d25304fcfca137420f14b)
- [REVIEW] Elder & Goldberg's ecological exponent is α = 2.92, close to Oyama's 2.89. Proximity reduced grouping-decision entropy by about 75%, and good continuation and similarity by about 10% each. Sigman et al. (2001) estimated only 0.6, but not restricted to single contours — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] Peterson & Gibson (1994), *Psychological Science, 5*, 253-259: in briefly shown two-region displays, symmetry and orientation-dependent recognition both influenced first-seen figure, and neither dominated. They propose recognition may operate on both sides of edges before figure assignment is settled, challenging the assumption that figure-ground must come first — [Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/must-figure-ground-organization-precede-object-recognition-an-ass/)
- [REVIEW] Border ownership has a neural correlate. About half of oriented V2 neurons are selective (Zhou, Friedman & von der Heydt 2000), and the context effect arrives less than 30 ms after earliest responses at 60-70 ms. Zhang & von der Heydt (2010) suggest feedback. Lamme (1995) found V1 figure-ground enhancement disappeared for figures larger than 8° — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] Part II treats holism, emergence, primacy of the whole, the minimum principle and self-organisation. It discusses operational definitions of holism such as configural superiority and global precedence, and three theoretical routes: complex self-organising systems, neural economy, and Bayesian optimality — [Wagemans et al. 2012 II](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3728284)

### Inferences
- The 1.5 ratio and the α near 2.9 describe specific stimuli (dot lattices, tangent elements in natural images). They suggest that relative spacing and not absolute pixels should drive grouping, but they do not validate a "1.5x gap" rule for cards or forms. A UI test would be needed.
- Additivity supports a practical thought: a similarity cue can buy back a spacing loss, within the tested range. It was shown for limited pairs of cues in regular patterns and is not proof that all cues add.
- Elder and Goldberg's 75% vs 10% is for contour-linking in natural images. It does not rank proximity over colour similarity in a layout.

### Gaps
- The Kubovy & van den Berg abstract and model parameters were not read directly.
- Part II's detail on Prägnanz, simplicity vs likelihood, configural superiority and microgenesis could not be read because of access blocks. [MEMORY] Relevant names for later checking are Hochberg & McAlister 1953, Leeuwenberg, van der Helm, Chater 1996, Pomerantz, and Kimchi.
- Peterson & Skow-Grant (2003) was not found in search.
- Numbers for good continuation, closure and symmetry on comparable scales were not found.

## Strength and competition: is there a hierarchy?

### Takeaway
I found no established universal ranking of Gestalt principles. Local, stimulus-dependent results exist: common region over connectedness in tested displays, proximity often strong, and colour often above shape. All are parameter-sensitive, as Wertheimer himself noted.

### Cited Findings
- [ABSTRACT] Quinlan & Wilton (1998), *Perception*: proximity had the strongest influence on grouping ratings, common shape gave only weak evidence, and colour was more persuasive. Both common colour and common shape could override proximity under certain conditions. The authors note the colour-shape contrast may reflect the particular values used — [Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p270417)
- [ABSTRACT] A later paper argues Quinlan & Wilton's result may reflect a shape-size confound — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/167/16732936036.pdf)
- [ABSTRACT] Montoro, Villalba-García, Luna & Hinojosa (2017), *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 24*, 1856-1861, DOI 10.3758/s13423-017-1254-3: using the repetition discrimination task, without explicit attention to grouping, common region dominated connectedness — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13423-017-1254-3.pdf)
- [ABSTRACT] A 2021 *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* study (Villalba-García et al., per a search summary) tested intrinsic cue competition (proximity vs luminance similarity) and extrinsic cue competition (common region vs connectedness), and reported common region dominance over connectedness — [Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/17470218211010486) (the page returned 403 when fetched; seen only through a search result)
- [REVIEW] Palmer & Rock's UC was proposed as earliest, but this is disputed (Peterson 1994; Kimchi 2000) — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [PRIMARY] Wertheimer showed similarity defeating proximity under particular spacing — [Wertheimer 1923/1938](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm)
- [REVIEW] Additive log-odds combination for proximity and similarity (Kubovy & van den Berg 2008) means results depend on the strength of each cue in the display — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)

### Inferences
- Any statement that "X always beats Y" misreads this literature. Better wording: "X beat Y at the values tested."
- A reasonable working heuristic for design, which is not an established law: use explicit containers or links when the grouping must be unambiguous, align spacing with them, and avoid cues that conflict. This is my inference from the additive and competition results.
- Explicit boundaries are extrinsic cues that add elements to the display, which may be why they are studied separately from intrinsic cues such as spacing and colour. This distinction is supported by the terminology of the 2021 study but the reason is my speculation.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis or full cross-principle ranking. Effect sizes for common region vs connectedness were not read.
- [MEMORY] Treating UC and connectedness as stronger than proximity and similarity is a common claim. I saw only a search summary stating UC can override both, with no effect sizes.

## Attention, development and culture

### Takeaway
"Pre-attentive grouping" is too blunt. Some groupings affect behaviour without awareness or attention, but reporting them needs attention, and demands vary across grouping types. Grouping develops at different ages for different principles. Cultural differences exist, but the strongest claims have mixed replication.

### Cited Findings
**Attention**
- [ABSTRACT] Mack, Tang, Tuma, Kahn & Rock (1992): observers in an attentionally demanding task could not report groupings in the unattended background, leading to the conclusion that the organisations were not perceived without attention — [Lamy et al. record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/grouping-does-not-require-attention/); [Razpurker-Apfeld & Kimchi 2007](https://iipdm.haifa.ac.il/images/publications/Rutie_Kimchi/Razpurker-Apfeld%20and%20Kimchi_2007_P&P.pdf)
- [ABSTRACT] Moore, C. M., & Egeth, H. (1997). Perception without attention: Evidence of grouping under conditions of inattention. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 23*(2), 339-352. Background dot patterns induced illusions affecting line judgments, although observers could not report the patterns. The authors concluded grouping can occur without attention but may not be encoded in memory — [Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Perception-without-attention-Evidence-of-grouping/9984002365102771)
- [ABSTRACT] Kimchi, R., & Razpurker-Apfeld, I. (2004). Perceptual grouping and attention: Not all groupings are equal. *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 11*(4), 687-696. Grouping is a set of processes that vary in attentional demand. Under inattention it is not all-or-none, and products of grouping are not available to awareness without attention — [Haifa record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/perceptual-grouping-and-attention-not-all-groupings-are-equal/)
- [ABSTRACT] Lamy, Segal & Ruderman (2006): grouping effects outside attention appear with implicit measures, not explicit report — [TAU record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/grouping-does-not-require-attention/)
- [ABSTRACT] Wood & Simons replicated the influence of grouping despite inattentional blindness online, without support for word-stem priming — [Illinois record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/processing-without-noticing-in-inattentional-blindness-a-replicat/)
- [ABSTRACT] Treisman (1982), *JEP:HPP, 8*, 194-214: grouping strongly affects conjunction search, with serial scanning between groups rather than items. Feature search appears independent of grouping — [Bohrium](https://www.bohrium.com/en/paper-details/perceptual-grouping-and-attention-in-visual-search-for-features-and-for-objects/812079371509563392-8299)
- [ABSTRACT] Ben-Av, Sagi & Braun (1992), *Perception & Psychophysics, 52*(3), 277-294, asks the same question with a concurrent task; only the question was seen — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758%2FBF03209145.pdf)

**Development**
- [ABSTRACT] Quinn, Bhatt, Brush, Grimes & Sharpnack (2002), *Psychological Science*: in familiarisation tests with arrays of Xs and Os, 6-7-month-olds but not 3-4-month-olds used form similarity. Later experiments ruled out processing time and discrimination failure. Different principles may become functional on different schedules — [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/1467-9280.00459/)
- [ABSTRACT] Quinn & Bhatt (2005) reported that 3-4-month-olds can use form similarity in a concept-formation task, suggesting that experience and task matter — [Quinn & Bhatt 2005](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/260290/mod_resource/content/0/Quinn_Bhatt_2005.pdf)
- [ABSTRACT] Secondary literature places luminance similarity earliest (neonates) and common fate by about 4 months. One longitudinal study found shape-similarity grouping at 4 months and proximity grouping at 8 months — [Farran et al. 2008](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1505916/1/FarranBrownetal2008ALongitudinal.pdf). Ages depend heavily on method, so treat as approximate.

**Culture and global precedence**
- [ABSTRACT] Navon-type tasks use target detection, while Kimchi-Palmer tasks use similarity judgment, and the two are not interchangeable — [Sex differences in Kimchi-Palmer task, 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7115960/)
- [ABSTRACT] Davidoff, Fonteneau & Fagot (2008), *Cognition, 108*(3), 702-709: Himba participants in northern Namibia showed a stronger local bias on hierarchical figures than any previously studied non-clinical population, attributed to culture and experience — [record](https://core.ac.uk/download/110888.pdf). Later work reports even brief exposure to a town reduced it — [PMC3198475](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3198475)
- [ABSTRACT] Chua, Boland & Nisbett (2005), *PNAS*: Americans fixated focal objects earlier and longer, Chinese participants more on context — [Boland page](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~jeboland/ChuaBolandNisbett05.pdf). Masuda & Nisbett (2001) showed Japanese and American participants underwater animations and collected descriptions — [PMC2668126](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2668126)
- [ABSTRACT] Rayner et al. (2007) did not replicate the eye-movement pattern, and Evans et al. (2009) conflicted. Boland, Chua & Nisbett (2008) responded that materials and task differed — [Boland et al. 2008](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~jeboland/BolandChuaNisbett08.pdf); [PMC4873513](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4873513/)

### Inferences
- Design text should say that grouping can shape performance without being noticed, but a user may not be able to report it or remember it. Automatic grouping and comprehension are not the same thing.
- Treisman's result gives the most direct route to UI search: groups can shape how search proceeds. It was measured on lab search, so extrapolate carefully.
- Culture findings justify caution about universal claims but do not justify locale-specific layout rules. Most of this evidence concerns attention to context, not grouping laws.
- "Global first" must not be stated as universal, given the Himba result and task dependence.

### Gaps
- [MEMORY] Driver, Davis, Russell, Turatto & Freeman (2001), "Segmentation, attention and phenomenal visual objects," *Cognition, 80*, and Russell & Driver (2005) were seen only by mention. Their findings are not established here.
- [MEMORY] Navon (1977), "Forest before trees," *Cognitive Psychology, 9*, was not opened, and reversal conditions were seen only in an unidentified search excerpt. Check Part II or Kimchi.
- Evans & Rotello (2013) could not be confirmed. Common region and connectedness in infants were not found.

## Limits, criticisms and likely design folklore

### Takeaway
The main scientific criticism is that classic Gestalt offered demonstrations and descriptions rather than mechanisms. Most UX-article errors come from overstating fixed lists, hierarchy, universality and numeric rules.

### Cited Findings
- [REVIEW] The review's listed criticisms of old-school Gestalt are mere demonstrations and subjective reports, simple or confounded stimuli, imprecise laws, a proliferation of a new "law" per factor, no mechanistic or neural explanation, and overextension to personality and social psychology — [Wagemans et al. 2012 I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)
- [ABSTRACT] Modern work has become quantitative and gives more weight to learning and non-image factors, and studies how principles combine — [Brooks 2015 abstract](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/35324)
- [ABSTRACT] Todorović asks where the principles come from, weighing past experience and the nervous system, and suggests they may work as heuristic cues to physical objects — [Humana.Mente](https://humanamente.eu/index.php/HM/issue/download/18/H.M.issue_17)
- [ABSTRACT] A 2022 paper argues similarity alone is incomplete and a dissimilarity principle may be more general — [PMC9326748](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9326748/)
- [ABSTRACT] Gestalt principles studied in a 2023 uncrowding paper did not give a clear explanation of the effect, an example of the limits of their explanatory power — [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2023.1154957/full)

**Folklore checklist.** The claim on the left is common in design writing. I did not systematically survey UX articles, so treat the prevalence as my judgment.

| Common claim | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| "The whole is greater than the sum of its parts" | Probably a misquote; verify | [MEMORY] Koffka; Part II unread. Note that Kubovy's 2008 title shows additive combination for some cues |
| Gestalt is a fixed list of 5-7 laws | False | Wertheimer's own list differs, and factors kept being added ([Wagemans I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/)) |
| Laws are innate and experience doesn't matter | False | [Table 2 of Wagemans I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/); [Quinn et al. 2002](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/1467-9280.00459/) |
| Proximity always beats similarity | False | [Wertheimer Sec. III](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Wertheimer/Forms/forms.htm); [Quinlan & Wilton](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/p270417) |
| Known total ranking of principles | Not established | Only pairwise, parameter-dependent results found |
| Grouping is fully pre-attentive | Too strong | [Kimchi & Razpurker-Apfeld 2004](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/perceptual-grouping-and-attention-not-all-groupings-are-equal/) |
| Gestalt is a universal visual truth | Too strong | [Davidoff et al. 2008](https://core.ac.uk/download/110888.pdf) |
| "Similarity equals 1.5 cm of proximity" or "1.5x spacing" rules | Unsupported for UI | [Rush critique and lattice conditions in Wagemans I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/) |
| Synchrony is a settled, independent principle | Contested | [Wagemans I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/) |
| A "Law of Prägnanz" is a precise predictive rule | Overstated | Imprecise-law criticism in [Wagemans I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/); Part II unread |

### Inferences
- Writers can safely say the following: spatial relations and visual properties bias grouping; cues combine and compete; strength depends on values; organisation influences behaviour, sometimes without report.
- Writers should avoid the following without separate evidence: rankings, conversion claims, universal rules and exact spacing ratios.
- Almost all of this evidence comes from sparse, brief, controlled lab displays. Web pages are dense, scrolled, task-driven and read. Extension to interfaces is plausible, but it needs its own HCI evidence, which another research stream should supply.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper surveying misuse of Gestalt in UX writing was found, so the checklist is my synthesis.
- The remaining Part I text (from about character 200,000), which includes neural mechanisms and later sections, was not read.
- Priority follow-ups for anyone with library access: Part II, Brooks 2015, Kubovy & van den Berg 2008, Koffka 1935, Palmer & Beck 2007, and Montoro et al. 2017 effect sizes.
